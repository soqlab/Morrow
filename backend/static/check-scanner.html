Free checker / Scanner identity

You found us in your logs.

Somebody used the free post-quantum readiness check at /check and typed your hostname. That produced one, or at most two, TLS handshakes to your server on port 443 and nothing else. This page says exactly what we do, so you can identify the traffic and decide what to do about it. The opt-out is unconditional and takes effect before DNS.

Make it stop

Ask never to be checked. No reason needed. Write to the address on the opt-out page — no abuse address is configured on this deployment yet.

What You Will Have Seen

One TCP connection to port 443, a complete TLS handshake with your hostname in the SNI, and then the socket closing with no request sent. Usually a second connection within a second or two, which sends one ClientHello and reads your ServerHello without completing the handshake — some stacks log that as a handshake failure or an incomplete connection, and that is what it is.

Your web server's access log probably holds nothing at all, because no HTTP request is ever sent to your host. If you were looking for a User-Agent in those logs, that is why there is none.

The facts in the table below are read from the code that runs this service, not from intention. The policy pages this links to (terms, acceptable use, privacy, abuse and opt-out) are drafts awaiting review by a qualified lawyer, and the egress address list below has not yet been filled in by the operator.

What We Connect To

BehaviourWhat the code does
PortsTCP 443 onlyThe port is not a setting. A configurable port would make this a port prober, which is the thing the guardrails exist to prevent, and a test asserts no such setting exists.
Connections per checkAt most 2, to one hostnameThe result published to the visitor states how many were actually made.
First connectionAn ordinary verified TLS handshakeCertificate verification on, hostname checking on, ALPN offering h2 and http/1.1 exactly as a browser does. We read the negotiated version, cipher, ALPN and your leaf certificate, then close. Nothing is sent after the handshake.
Second connectionOne ClientHello, offering post-quantum key-exchange groupsWe read the ServerHello to see which group you chose, then close. The handshake is never completed and no application data is ever sent. It is attempted only if something answered on 443, and only if the check has time left.
HTTP requests to youNone, everNo GET, no HEAD, no robots.txt fetch, no crawl.
RedirectsNever followedThere is no HTTP request to you to redirect. Our own requests to the Certificate Transparency service are made with redirects disabled.
Address pinningThe address we checked is the address we connect toThe hostname is resolved once, every answer must be a globally routable address, and both connections go to that one address while SNI and certificate verification still use the hostname. A second DNS answer cannot redirect us.
Never contactedAnything that is not a public hostIP literals, wildcards, credentials before the host, single-label names, any port other than 443, and names that resolve to private, loopback, link-local (including 169.254.169.254), carrier-grade NAT, reserved, multicast or unspecified addresses, in IPv4 and IPv6, with IPv4-mapped and 6to4 forms unwrapped first. A name that resolves to one public and one private address is refused outright.
Not done at allNo port scanning, no subdomain discovery, no vulnerability probing, no authentication attempts, no crawling, no OCSP or CRL fetch, no TLS-version or cipher enumerationThe platform's full scanner does several of those. This service deliberately never calls it.

Making It Stop

Opt out (preferred)

Write to the abuse address and name your domain. It is then refused before its name is resolved, for every name under it, and the cached result is deleted. Nothing reaches your network at all. See abuse and opt-out.

Block us

You can, and nobody here objects. Once the egress list above is published it will be there for exactly this purpose. A blocked connection produces a result reported as failed, with no verdict and no score — a failed check never reads as a clean one.

Ask what happened

The operator can look up every check attempted against your domain: the times, the outcomes including refusals, and a salted hash per requester. The requester's IP address is not stored and cannot be recovered from the hash.

Why This Traffic Exists

Post-quantum migration deadlines are published and dated, and most people have no idea what their own TLS endpoint negotiates. The free check answers that in a few seconds with every figure sourced, and it is the shop window for the paid platform. Reading a public TLS handshake is the same thing a browser and SSL Labs do; that does not entitle us to your traffic if you would rather we left you alone, which is why the opt-out needs no reason and takes effect before DNS.

The rules the people using it are held to are on the acceptable-use policy: no automation, no enumeration, no using it to map someone else's attack surface. What is recorded about you, and published, is in the privacy notice.
