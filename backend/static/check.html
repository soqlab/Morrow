Is your TLS quantum-vulnerable?

Type a domain. You get what its public endpoint negotiates today — protocol, cipher, certificate key, signature, and whether the key exchange is already post‑quantum — plus what the published migration deadlines mean for it. One endpoint, one moment, public data only, and every figure says where it came from.

A hostname, nothing else. No ports, no paths, no IP addresses, no wildcards.

Running a check means you accept the terms of use and the acceptable‑use policy: check hosts you own, a supplier you are assessing, or a domain you are researching in good faith — not someone else's attack surface, and not from a script. There is no account here, so acceptance works on use. If you operate a domain and never want it checked, opt out.

What this contacts

Public Certificate Transparency logs for the domain — passive, the target is not touched. Then at most two connections to the hostname you typed, on port 443: one ordinary TLS handshake, and one that offers post‑quantum key‑exchange groups so hybrid support can be observed.

No port scanning. No subdomain discovery. No vulnerability probing. No crawling. Nothing private, loopback or reserved, ever.

        Rules for using it

          Abuse reports and opt‑out requests. If this service has been pointed at something it should not have been, or you operate a domain and do not want it checked here, write to the address published on our contact page. An opted‑out domain is refused before anything is resolved or connected to, and its cached result is deleted. The opt‑out covers the domain and everything under it.

          Every check is logged: the domain, the time, a salted hash of the requester's address, and the outcome. That is what makes an abuse report investigable. It is described in the privacy notice for this checker, and nothing here is stored against a customer account.

          The full documents: terms of use · acceptable‑use policy · abuse and opt‑out · privacy notice · scanner identity. They are drafts awaiting review by a qualified lawyer. The statement above is served live from /api/public-check/config, so it cannot drift from the running code; if it and the policy pages ever disagree, this one describes the software.
