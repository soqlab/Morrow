### Added
- Board slide in one click: a **Board slide** button on the console's Overview (next to Why it matters) and in Board & ROI downloads one 16:9 slide for a board pack, as PowerPoint (editable text, the chart drawn as shapes with its data as alt text, no images) or PDF. One number dominates: by when the most sensitive data in scope must be quantum-safe, from the harvest clock (Mosca's inequality: the Global Risk Institute's 2035 to 2040 estimate minus how long the data must stay private, from the Shelf-Life kind of data picked for the risk sentence or a longer numeric confidentiality lifetime in the inventory), always labelled an estimate with its range; the organization's risk sentence (services/risk_sentence.py over the newest scan of each endpoint); the most honest trend there is (the measured quantum-safe share of scanned endpoints at each month end, else the fire drill's estimated time-to-safe with ranges, else "not measured yet"); and one to three next actions from the fix queue. Every figure has its source and date in the footer, and nothing is filled in where data is missing. Any signed-in member of the organization can download it, for their own organization only; each download is one audit entry (`board_slide.export`, the format, no content); a support view cannot. `GET /board-slide` (JSON), `GET /board-slide/export.pptx`, `GET /board-slide/export.pdf` (services/board_slide.py; the PPTX is written with the standard library, the PDF with reportlab, so no new dependency).
- Wall mode (`/wall`, services/display_links.py): one sentence and one number on an office TV or monitor, refreshed every minute. The sentence is the organization's quantum risk sentence (services/risk_sentence.py); the number is the admin's pick per screen: hosts not yet quantum-safe, the share of hosts that are quantum-safe, or the latest fire drill's time-to-safe. A small footer says when the screen last updated and how old the data is; when Morrow can't be reached the screen keeps the last real values, dims them and says "Can't reach Morrow. Last updated 3 hours ago", and with nothing measured it shows a dash, never a zero. Nobody signs in on the screen: an admin makes a display link in Administration, Display links (name, number, optional expiry, dark or light), sees who made each one and when it was last used, and revokes it at once. The link carries a random token in the URL fragment; the page sends it only as the `X-Display-Token` header to `GET /api/public/wall` (ETag / If-None-Match, 304 when nothing changed; backs off to 15 minutes on errors), and the token opens nothing else. Only its SHA-256 is stored. The page loads nothing from another origin, is noindex and sends no Referer; it is high contrast, scales from a phone to 4K, and shifts a few pixels every few minutes against burn-in unless reduced motion is on. Creating, changing and revoking a link are audited. `GET/POST /display-links`, `PATCH/DELETE /display-links/{id}`. `display_links` is org-scoped and in the RLS plan.
- Quantum fire drill (services/fire_drill.py), at the top of the console's Remediation view: "If X25519 were broken this morning, the 14 systems that depend on it would be on X25519MLKEM768 in about 9 days (somewhere between 4 and 31 days)." A drill runs the crypto kill switch's own planner (`crypto_switch.build_plan`: policy pre-flight, every affected asset from the newest scans, its owner and the change for its stack) against the organization's real inventory, always in dry run: it declares no incident, opens no pull request, requests no rescan and sends nothing; its only writes are its own record and one audit entry. Time-to-safe is the slowest owner lane: each owner's response time (measured from the organization's merged remediations and kill-switch requests where there are any, otherwise a stated, wide assumption) plus the playbook's hands-on time for each change applied by hand; an asset with a linked repository and a connected code host goes by pull request; a CDN or cloud load balancer that does not offer the fallback is a lane of its own. No owner, no history, never-scanned assets, unconfirmed key sizes and stale scans are listed and widen the range instead of moving the number. Every drill is stored, so the console shows the trend ("down from 24 days last quarter"). Admins and owners run a drill now or pick a cadence (off, monthly, quarterly; Celery beat `morrow.fire_drill_due`, hourly); members can view. `GET/POST /fire-drills`, `GET /fire-drills/{id}`, `GET/PUT /fire-drills/schedule`. `fire_drills` and `fire_drill_schedules` are org-scoped and in the RLS plan.
- Ask your assistant (self-hosted, docs/ASK_YOUR_ASSISTANT.md): `morrow-mcp` (tools/morrow_mcp) is a read-only MCP server that runs on the user's machine over stdio, so their own AI assistant can answer "what's our quantum risk?" without the console. Six tools, each a GET with the organization's API key under the same scoping and role checks as the console: a domain's risk sentence and harvest clock (from the newest stored grade: the new `?stored=1` on `GET /api/public/grade/{domain}` never starts a check), the inventory in numbers with recent scans, findings needing action, Make it safe status and proof, and grade changes. On the server (services/mcp_access.py) a request marked `X-Client: mcp/<tool>` is answered only with `MORROW_MCP_ENABLED=1` (off unless set; `0` in render.yaml, so the hosted service refuses), only with a key whose scopes are exactly `read` (write, admin and sign keys, sessions, support tokens and header identities are refused with a sentence saying to create a read-only key), and only for GET/HEAD; each call, allowed or denied, is appended to the organization's hash-chained audit journal as `mcp.tool_call` with the tool, key id, method, path and status, never the key, query string or answer. Answers are bounded (about 12,000 characters, 2 MB read) and control characters are removed
- Your quantum risk in one sentence, at the top of every report (the public check at /<domain>, the instrument's /scan and /estate/asset views, and the console's scan results): "Your personal data sent to api.example.com today could be read from about 2035 (expert estimates: 2035 to 2040), but it needs to stay private until 2096. Fix 2 settings and traffic from then on can't be read with a quantum computer." Built once in services/risk_sentence.py from the harvest clock (decrypt_window.harvest, CRQC_ESTIMATE), the Shelf-Life kind of data and the settings that fix it, served at `GET /api/public/risk-sentence`. It says which kind of data it assumed and lets the person change it in place (the harvest clock follows); a host that is already safe, unencrypted or unreached gets that answer instead. The public grade now lists `settings_to_fix` and carries `risk`.
- Live safe badge: the owner of a domain claimed by DNS turns it on in the domain's owner tools and pastes one line of HTML. `GET /api/public/safe-badge/<domain>.svg` shows "Quantum-safe | verified <date>" only while Morrow's newest check (at most 48 hours old) is grade A or B with a post-quantum key exchange, and the claim is still held by the organization that turned it on; otherwise it quietly shows "Morrow | quantum check" and claims nothing. Badged domains are re-checked daily by the watch tick (same guarded probe, limits and scanner User-Agent). The image reads stored data only, sets no cookies, loads nothing else, is not written to the traffic monitor, and links to the domain's signed quantum-safe proof (`/r/<domain>/proof`); the domain's public page states the badge's state too. `safe_badges` is org-scoped and in the RLS plan. The privacy page says what a badge request leaves behind.
- Make it safe (services/make_safe.py, docs in the module): on `/<domain>`, on a signed-in endpoint (`/estate/asset/<key>`) and from the console's scan results, every host that is not quantum-safe gets one action. The fix: Morrow names what terminates TLS (Cloudflare, CloudFront, AWS ALB/NLB, API Gateway, Azure Front Door, Application Gateway, Google Cloud Load Balancing, Fastly, Akamai, nginx, Apache, HAProxy, Caddy, Traefik, IIS) from the grade's Server and fingerprinting headers, the CNAME chain, the CDNs' published address ranges and the certificate issuer, with its confidence and evidence, and shows the change for that stack with minimum versions. Every recipe lives in `backend/data/make_safe_recipes.json` with the vendor's own URL, a quote and the date it was checked; where a vendor documents no support (Application Gateway) or the stack is unknown, it says so and gives the next step instead of a setting. The watch: "I've made the change" asks for spaced re-checks through the public grade (30 s doubling to 15 min, ten automatic tries, paused while the tab is hidden; 429 with Retry-After before the next slot) and the row turns green when it is live. The proof: `/r/<domain>/proof` (and `/r/<domain>/proof.json`) is a signed "quantum-safe since <date>" statement derived from stored grades, signed with the deployment's Ed25519 + ML-DSA-65 proof keys under its own signing context, checkable in the browser or offline against `/.well-known/morrow-proof-keys.json`; it says "no longer quantum-safe" once a later check sees a classical handshake, and states a DNS claim and its date but never who holds it. "Make it safe" is now the primary button on `/r/<domain>`; the grade keeps a short list of fingerprinting response headers (`edge_headers`); the IIS fix no longer cites an update number Microsoft does not document
- Shelf-Life in the harvest clock: the example buttons ("25 y: health records") are replaced by a picker for the kind of data and whose law, from the Shelf-Life v0.1 catalogue (SOQLAB and contributors, CC BY 4.0, https://github.com/soqlab/shelf-life; 51 entries, 13 categories, EU/UK/US/DE/CA/AU and one jurisdiction-neutral entry), vendored unchanged in `backend/data/shelf-life/` with its checksum and a NOTICE. `services/shelf_life.py` verifies the SHA-256 (published file and a pinned value) before use and falls back to the old examples when it does not match; `GET /api/public/shelf-life` serves it trimmed. The dial is set to the entry's upper bound, the range, basis ("set by law" or "reasoned estimate, low confidence") and, in expert depth, the legal citations are shown, and the sentence quotes it: "UK health records need to stay private for at least 50 years, up to 100 (Shelf-Life v0.1). Traffic captured today could be read around 2040 — that's 36 to 86 years too early." The jurisdiction defaults from the domain's country TLD, else the visitor's last choice on the device; links carry `?data=health&jur=GB`. The dial now reaches 130 years (`decrypt_window.SHELF_YEARS_MAX`), finer at the short end. `/about-scanner` lists where the numbers come from, with the attribution

### Fixed
- Demoting, deactivating or removing someone now takes effect on their very next request, and their account and membership no longer disagree. Every role change, deactivation, reactivation and removal (`PUT /auth/users/{id}/role`, `POST /auth/users/{id}/deactivate`, `POST|PATCH|DELETE /organizations/{id}/members[/{user}]`, `PUT /admin/users/role`, `DELETE /admin/users` and the `/platform/customers/{id}/members|users` routes) goes through one function (services/member_access.py) that writes the sign-in account and the organization membership together (and the invitation record when there is one), and, when access goes down, revokes the person's access tokens, deletes their refresh sessions on a deactivation or removal and revokes the API keys they created above their new role. Before, `PUT /auth/users/{id}/role` changed only the account and the membership routes only the membership, and nothing touched tokens already issued, so a demoted or deactivated admin kept admin rights until their token expired (up to 24 hours). The membership is now the authoritative record: the console's admin routes read it (and the account) on every request instead of trusting the token's role claim, and a disabled membership has no role. Promotions apply on the next request without revoking anything. Journaled `member_change.applied` and audited `auth.member.<role|deactivate|reactivate|remove>`. At startup, accounts and memberships that disagree are brought into line toward the lesser access, once and idempotently, each fix logged by organization and account id. Built-in tokens carry `iat_ms`, so signing in again straight after a revocation is not caught by it. Nobody deactivates or removes themselves when that would leave the organization with no active admin (`last_admin`, 403), which an organization without a recorded owner allowed
- Flaky tests: `test_pq_signing` sent "now + 1 second" truncated to the second, which the server (also truncating) could see as "now"; `tests/test_demo_mode.py` and `test_db_connection_lifetimes.py` were hit by daemon threads left by earlier tests (scan workers, the scheduler, journal and audit writers) calling `db.get_connection()`, which reads `MORROW_SCANNER_DB` on every call and builds the schema in whatever file it names. The demo purge test no longer moves that variable, and the lifetime test only tracks its own thread's connections
- TLS scanner: TLS 1.0 and 1.1 were never detected under OpenSSL 3 (the probes failed locally at the default security level); a TLS 1.2 server with only RSA key exchange was reported as not speaking TLS 1.2 and its certificate was not read
- TLS scanner: hybrid key exchange was missed on servers that accept an offered X25519 share instead of asking for X25519MLKEM768 (Fastly, Akamai, Mozilla and others); the probe now sends a browser-shaped X25519MLKEM768 key share
- Harvest-now-decrypt-later verdict now follows the observed key exchange: an endpoint that negotiates a hybrid ML-KEM group is no longer reported "critical"/"high"
